# Executive Summary

## Objective

The Customer Segmentation & Churn Pattern Analytics project supports retail banking decision-making by identifying which customer groups are most likely to leave the bank and why.

## Main Findings

- Churn risk is not uniform across the customer base. It clusters around geography, age, activity status, tenure, and balance profile.
- Inactive members have significantly higher churn risk than active members.
- Customers with lower tenure and lower engagement are at greater risk of exit in the early life cycle.
- Higher-balance customers can represent material revenue risk if they churn, even if their proportion in the customer base is smaller.

## Strategic Implications

1. Retention initiatives should focus on recently acquired and less active customers.
2. Country- and segment-specific strategies are more effective than one-size-fits-all campaigns.
3. Premium segment monitoring is essential to reduce revenue leakage.
4. Regular churn tracking should be incorporated into management reporting.

## Recommended Actions

- Launch preventive outreach for inactive members.
- Segment retention offers by age, geography, and balance level.
- Create a high-value customer retention policy.
- Use the dashboard to monitor churn KPIs continuously.

## Final Message

This initiative provides a practical and evidence-based foundation for reducing churn in European banking. By prioritizing the highest-risk segments, banks can protect customer lifetime value and improve strategic planning.
